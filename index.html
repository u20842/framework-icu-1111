The Foundation

The Root Thesis

Every pattern that shows up in relationships, health, achievement, and self-worth is not random. Not a personality flaw. Not a character defect.

It is the predictable output of an identity that was never fully formed.

Or one formed under conditions that required distortion in order to survive. The work here is not symptom management. It is identity completion — a fully formed self that doesn't need to prove its worth, borrow its worth, or perform its worth.

The Diagnostic

The PAST Framework™

A diagnostic system — not a judgment. It identifies the four patterns that emerge from incomplete identity. Each one is a survival strategy that outlived its usefulness — the self's attempt to manage one root wound: I am worthless.

P
The Pairing
Wounds Recognize Wounds

Not chemistry or compatibility — complementary wound management. One extreme assigns blame outward. The other receives it inward. Neither is choosing consciously. Both are choosing by wound.

One needs someone to hold the blame. One needs somewhere to put it. The relationship functions as a shared reality-management system — until the Squeeze.

A
The Audition
Performing for Love

When love was conditional in childhood, we learn to audition. Not to show up — to perform. To give from fear rather than fullness.

Every act of giving is also an act of earning. Presence is never available, because you are always rehearsing. And no performance secures worth permanently.

S
The Squeeze
Pressure Reveals Truth

Under pressure, we don't rise to our intentions — we fall to our patterns. The Squeeze is the moment the defense system fails and reality breaks through. Crisis, conflict, illness, loss, rupture.

The Squeeze is not a failure. It is an initiation — the moment the framework becomes available, because the distortion finally cracked enough to let something true in.

T
The Tank
What You Carry Is What You Pour

You cannot give structural worth to someone else until you stop performing it and stop borrowing it. The tank has been running on performance and approval — never on structural worth. It was never full.

What pours from a tank that runs on performance is more performance. Filling the tank is not self-care as commodity — it is identity completion.

The Solution

The DEAL Framework™

The DEAL Framework doesn't prescribe the same action for both extremes. The instruction is the same — but what it requires looks completely different depending on which direction you're coming from. That's what makes it clinically precise rather than generically motivational.

"Problems aren't the problem. How you DEAL with them is."

D
Don't Resist Reality
The First Honest Look

Every defense in the PAST Framework is resistance to reality. D is the intervention for both extremes — from their own side of it.

Applied dually

Self-authorship: Let it land. You were wrong. You're still here. Being wrong did not kill you.

Empathy: See yourself. You've been absent from your own life. Being seen will not kill you either.

E
Embrace Discomfort
The Swing Itself

Where the shame lives — and where the pendulum swings. The overcorrection. This is why the work feels worse before it feels better, and why most people quit here.

Applied dually

Self-authorship: Sitting with "I was wrong" without converting it to blame.

Empathy: Having a preference in public. Saying no. The word is not selfish — the word is present.

A
Adjust Perspective
Where the Swing Slows

Where the overcorrection calibrates and the plain true thing becomes available. You were wrong and you are okay. You exist and you are allowed. Neither the greatest nor the worst.

The reframe

Not positive thinking. Accuracy. The distortion was inaccurate in both directions. A is the return to what is simply true.

L
Level Up
The Return to Center

Not an achievement — a return. Not to the original position, not to the opposite extreme. To the middle. Structural worth. Just being. Present.

The destination

The integrated person can be wrong without being worthless. Can be rejected without being erased. Can hold shame and own it or set it down.

Three ways of relating to your own worth

Performance-based worth: I must generate proof. Borrowed worth: I must collect it from others. Structural worth: I simply am it.

Three relationships with shame

A fully formed identity doesn't choose between knowing what you want and feeling what others need. It integrates both — through a boundary that discerns rather than defends. Unchecked, each pole collapses into its extreme.
